GOAL RULES — these override everything else:
Fat loss goal: Low calorie density foods that fill the stomach. High protein to preserve muscle. No full cream milk — use low fat or skim. No peanut butter as a staple — maximum one tablespoon if budget allows. No avocado unless flexible budget. Egg whites over whole eggs where possible but whole eggs acceptable for budget clients. Sweet potato over white pap always. High volume vegetables at every meal — cabbage, spinach, morogo, broccoli when affordable. Lean proteins only — pilchards, egg whites, chicken breast not thigh, tuna, low fat cottage cheese. No added oils where possible — grill, boil, steam.
Muscle gain goal: Higher calorie density. Whole eggs always — the yolk has the nutrients. Full cream milk if budget allows — the extra calories and fat support muscle growth. Peanut butter is encouraged — calorie dense and protein rich. Sweet potato and oats as primary carbs for sustained energy. Red meat twice a week if budget allows for iron and creatine. Larger portions. Pre and post workout meals are mandatory — carb and protein before, protein and carb after.
Recomposition goal: Moderate calories. Carb timing matters — carbs before and after training, lower carbs in evening meals. High protein at every meal without exception. Lean proteins as primary source. Healthy fats in moderation — one tablespoon peanut butter, one egg yolk, small amount of olive oil if budget allows. No empty calories at all — no cool drinks, no sweets, no processed foods.
General fitness goal: Balanced. No extreme restrictions. Educate on portions rather than eliminate foods. Sustainable eating that can be maintained for life.
LIFE SITUATION RULES — these change the entire structure:
Student: Maximum 15 minutes prep time per meal. Maximum 3 ingredients per meal. Res and tuck shop acknowledged — give strategies for eating in res not just home cooking. Maggi noodles with an egg is a legitimate student meal. Microwave meals must be included as options.
Domestic worker: Eats in employer's home often. Give strategy — protein first on any plate, skip extra starch, choose fruit over biscuits if available. Home meals must be under 20 minutes and under 5 ingredients. Batch cooking on Sunday for the week.
Night shift worker: Meal timing completely restructured. Pre-shift meal replaces breakfast regardless of clock time. Post-shift meal replaces dinner. One meal during shift if allowed. Sleep quality meals — low sugar, no caffeine after mid-shift.
Unemployed: Maximum value per rand. Every meal must cost under R15. Bulk cooking — big pot of beans or stew that feeds 3 days. No waste meals — use everything. Pap is acceptable in correct portions because it is cheap and filling.
Physically active job: Higher calorie needs than sedentary clients at same weight. Add 300 calories to the target. More carbohydrates to fuel physical work. Recovery foods in the evening — protein plus anti-inflammatory foods like spinach and sweet potato.
MEDICAL CONDITION RULES:
Diabetic: Every carb must be low GI. White pap replaced with samp and beans or oats or sweet potato or brown rice. Meal timing is medication — breakfast 7am, snack 10am, lunch 12pm, snack 3pm, dinner 6pm. Never skip. Never merge meals. Portion sizes consistent day to day — blood sugar management requires consistency. High fibre at every meal — beans, vegetables, oats. No fruit juice — whole fruit only and maximum one serving per meal. No white bread — low GI bread or whole wheat only. Flag: if on Metformin take with food always.
Hypertension: Sodium is the enemy. No Aromat, no stock cubes, no instant noodles, no processed meats — polony, Russians, viennas all removed from the plan entirely. Season with fresh herbs, lemon juice, garlic, pepper. Potassium rich foods actively included — banana, sweet potato, spinach, beans — these actively lower blood pressure. Magnesium rich foods — oats, nuts if budget allows, dark leafy greens. Limit red meat to once per week.
PCOS: Low GI identical to diabetic protocol. Anti-inflammatory foods prioritised — fatty fish like pilchards and tuna, leafy greens, berries when budget allows. Reduce processed foods entirely. Consistent meal timing. Higher protein to manage insulin resistance. Avoid seed oils — cook in small amount of coconut oil or dry cook where possible.
HIV on ARVs: Higher protein needs — add 20 percent to protein target. Higher calorie needs — add 200 calories to target. ARVs must be taken with food — flag which meal to take them with. Immune supporting foods — vitamin C rich foods like tomato, spinach, orange when affordable. No raw foods that could cause infection risk — cook everything thoroughly.
Allergies from otherMedicalNotes: Parse the stored allergy text. If peanut — remove all peanut butter, replace with sunflower seed butter if budget flexible or extra eggs if budget tight. If tree nuts — remove all nuts. If fish — remove pilchards and tuna, replace with eggs and chicken as primary proteins.
